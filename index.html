    Guides & best practices

    Short, practical reading to get more out of every tool.

    Image compression & SEO: faster pages, better rankings

    Page speed is a confirmed ranking signal, and images usually make up the largest share of a page's weight. Compressing them is the quickest win for Core Web Vitals, especially Largest Contentful Paint, which is very often an image. As a rule of thumb, photographs rarely need more than 75–85% quality: the difference is invisible to most viewers, yet file size can drop by 60% or more.

    WebP typically beats JPEG by 25–35% at comparable quality and supports transparency, which makes it the best default for the modern web, while PNG remains useful for sharp-edged graphics, screenshots and logos. Resizing matters as much as compression: serving a 4,000-pixel photo into an 800-pixel column wastes bandwidth. Match the maximum width to the largest size at which the image is really displayed, and allow 2× for high-density screens. Always add descriptive file names and alt text so search engines understand the picture. Because WadedeTools Pro compresses inside your browser, your photos are never uploaded, which makes it safe for client work, unreleased products and personal pictures.

    QR code best practices that actually scan

    A QR code is only useful if it scans on the first try. Keep strong contrast between the dots and the background and prefer dark dots on a light background, because many scanners struggle with inverted or low-contrast colors. Leave a quiet zone, an empty margin of at least four modules, around the code, and never print it smaller than about 2 × 2 cm for close-range scanning. The more data you encode, the denser the pattern becomes, so use short URLs and avoid unnecessary parameters.

    If you add a center logo, error correction must increase: level H can recover roughly 30% of a damaged code, which is why this tool switches to it automatically when a logo is present. Always test the finished code with at least two different phones before printing, and test it again at its final size and on its final material. For Wi-Fi codes, remember that anyone who scans can join your network, so use a guest network where possible. Static codes are permanent, so double-check the destination before you publish, and export SVG for print because vector graphics stay perfectly sharp at any size.

    Text optimization for readers, search and AI

    Good copy is written for people first and search engines second. Use the live statistics to stay on target: meta titles perform best at around 50–60 characters and meta descriptions at around 150–160, while long-form articles that fully answer a question often run between 1,200 and 2,000 words. Reading time helps set expectations, and an average adult reads about 200–250 words per minute.

    The keyword density panel shows which terms dominate your draft. Natural repetition of your main topic is healthy, but if a single word accounts for a large share of the text it may read as stuffed, so vary your vocabulary with synonyms and related phrases. Short paragraphs, descriptive headings and one idea per sentence improve readability on mobile screens. When you publish, create clean, lowercase, hyphen-separated URL slugs that contain the main keyword, and strip stray formatting, double spaces and invisible characters that can break layouts. The same tidy, consistent text also gives AI assistants cleaner input and better results.

    Password security: length, uniqueness and randomness

    Password strength comes from length and unpredictability, not clever substitutions. Every extra character multiplies the number of guesses an attacker needs, so a random 16-character password is vastly stronger than a short one full of symbols. Use a unique password for every account, because credential stuffing reuses leaked passwords across sites. Humans are poor random number generators; this tool uses your browser's cryptographically secure random source, the same kind of randomness used for encryption keys, and nothing is ever sent over the network.

    Store passwords in a reputable password manager instead of memorizing them or keeping them in a text file, and protect the manager with a long passphrase and two-factor authentication. Prefer authenticator apps or hardware security keys over SMS codes. The "time to crack" figure is an estimate that assumes an offline attack of ten billion guesses per second against a fast hash; real systems with slow hashing and rate limits are much harder to attack, but it is wise to plan for the worst case. Change a password immediately if a service reports a breach, and never share it by email or chat.

    Frequently asked questions

    Are my files uploaded to a server?

    No. Every tool runs entirely inside your browser using JavaScript and built-in web APIs. Your images, text, QR content and passwords are never transmitted, stored or seen by us.

    Is WadedeTools Pro really free?

    Yes. All four tools are free to use with no sign-up, no watermarks and no daily limits. The site is supported by non-intrusive advertising.

    Which image format should I choose?

    WebP is the best all-round choice: it is typically 25–35% smaller than JPEG and supports transparency. Use JPEG for maximum compatibility with photos, and PNG for sharp graphics, screenshots or logos where lossless quality matters.

    Are the generated passwords stored or sent anywhere?

    No. Passwords and UUIDs are generated locally with your browser's cryptographically secure random number generator and exist only in your device's memory until you close or refresh the page.

    Do the QR codes I create expire?

    No. These are static QR codes: the information is encoded directly in the pattern, so they never expire, never track scans and keep working as long as the destination, such as a website, stays online.

    Does it work on phones and tablets?

    Yes. The interface is fully responsive and works in any modern browser on desktop, tablet and mobile, including paste and drag-and-drop where your device supports them.
